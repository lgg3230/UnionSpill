# LPM Survival Results — Theory Discussion
*Five senior economists, April 2026*

## The empirical pattern

**Direct effect** (firms with CBAs affected by Súmula 277):
- Pre-treatment incumbents: +1.3 to +1.7 pp annual survival probability post-2012
- Pre-trend passes (p = 0.26–0.59), consistent across all panels

**Spillover effect** (non-treated firms connected via pre-treatment worker flows):
- Full sample: coefficient insignificant (0.002), pre-trend FAILS (p = 0.000) — contaminated by post-reform entry of new connected firms
- Pre-treatment incumbents only: −1.1 pp annual survival probability post-2012
- Pre-trend passes (p = 0.82)

---

## Where all five agree

1. **The direct effect is clean.** Treated firms survive better because the reform raised their attractiveness as employers and reduced cost uncertainty (ultractivity locks in a floor).
2. **The core spillover mechanism is competitive wage pressure.** The reform improved outside options for workers at connected firms, forcing those firms to raise wages without the institutional support or productivity offset that treated firms received through bargaining. Marginal connected firms exit because they cannot absorb the cost squeeze.
3. **The full-sample spillover pre-trend failure is not fatal** — it reflects entry of new connected firms post-reform. The pre-treatment incumbent restriction recovers clean identification.

---

## Individual positions

### Prof. Marchetti — Union/Collective Bargaining Specialist
**Primary mechanism: quasi-rent compression.**
Connected firms were extracting monopsony rents pre-reform. The reform eroded those rents via competitive outside-options pressure, and the least profitable exit. Ultractivity simultaneously raises treated firms' costs *and* reduces their cost variance — they get a predictable, negotiated cost structure. Connected untreated firms get only the cost side.

**Key prediction:** Exiting connected firms should show *rising wages right up to closure* (not workforce rundown) if cost squeeze is the driver. A workforce rundown before exit would instead point to labor drain.

---

### Prof. Hsieh — Firm Dynamics / IO
**Primary mechanism: general equilibrium labor market reallocation.**
Agrees on cost squeeze but adds a distinct second channel: **labor supply depletion**. Treated firms, now better employers, *retain* workers who would previously have cycled into connected firms. Connected firms face an adverse labor supply shock — not just higher wages, but a shrunken hiring pool. Frames the whole result as Melitz-style selection: the exit margin reveals what the balanced-panel wage regressions hide, since surviving connected firms are positively selected.

The full-sample pre-trend failure is itself informative: post-reform entrants into the connected network are firms willing to compete in the new environment — positively selected on survival. They mask the exit of incumbents.

---

### Prof. Ferreira — Development / Brazil / Informality
**Primary mechanism: cost squeeze + informality margin.**
Agrees on competitive wage pressure, but raises a mechanism the others do not: some "firm exit" in RAIS may be **transition to informality** rather than firm death. The reform tips marginal formal firms across the formality threshold. If so, the −1.1 pp is partly a welfare loss for workers (shifting to informal employment with lower protection) rather than pure productive destruction.

Also emphasizes the asymmetry of institutional support: treated firms get higher wages *and* a stable institutional structure (ultractivity, negotiated amenities); connected untreated firms absorb only the cost with no offsetting institutional arrangement.

---

### Prof. Moreau — Worker Mobility / Sorting
**Primary mechanism: worker quality cream-skimming.**
Even with null average separation rates, connected firms may lose their *best* workers disproportionately to better-paying treated firms. In an AKM framework, the reform shifted treated firms up the employer wage premium distribution. This makes them differentially attractive to high-value workers. Losing key workers is enough to tip marginal connected firms toward exit even without large aggregate flow effects. Consistent with the paper's finding that high-skill connectivity predicts larger wage responses.

Note: null turnover effects in the paper are estimated on the balanced panel (survivors). The exit margin is precisely the firms excluded from that panel — so turnover results cannot rule out this channel for exiting firms.

---

### Prof. Katz — Applied Econometrician (Skeptic)
**Structural story (if causal):** Product market competition interacting with the labor cost channel. Connected firms compete with treated firms for both workers *and* customers. They face higher wage floors without the bargaining structure to manage them, shrinking margins and raising exit probability.

**Identification concerns:**

1. **Pre-existing decline.** High-connectivity firms may have been on secular decline that a 3-year pre-trend window cannot detect. The treatment effect is estimated over 4 post-treatment years — more time for latent heterogeneity to express itself than the pre-trend test covers.

2. **Survivor bias in the main wage results.** The balanced-panel wage spillover (+0.51%) excludes exiting connected firms. If those firms would have shown *negative* wage effects, the headline elasticity is upward-biased. The direct and spillover results are consistent but not yet formally reconciled.

## Exercises proposed to adjudicate

| Exercise | Proposed by | What it distinguishes |
|---|---|---|
| Test whether exiting connected firms show rising wages before closure vs. workforce rundown | Marchetti | Cost squeeze vs. labor drain |
| Check whether high-turnover connected firms drive the exit effect | Hsieh, Ferreira | Labor competition channel |
| Check whether exit is concentrated among low-wage connected firms | Ferreira | Informality margin vs. cost squeeze |
| Trace worker flows from exiting connected firms to treated firms in 2–3 years pre-exit | Moreau | Cream-skimming / quality sorting |
| Compare pre-treatment employment/wage *growth rates* of exiting vs. surviving connected incumbents | Katz | Pre-existing decline threat |
| Reweight balanced-panel wage results by survival probability (IPW) | Katz | Survivor bias in main wage result |
| Shift-share IV: instrument connectivity with treatment density in industry-microregion cell × baseline mobility | Katz | Confounding between connectivity and pre-existing firm health |

---

## Key unresolved disagreement

**Katz vs. the other four.** Katz is skeptical that the −1.1 pp is causal without ruling out pre-existing decline. The other four find the passing pre-trend on incumbents sufficient.

**Most tractable resolution:** Compare pre-treatment employment and wage *growth rates* between eventually-exiting and surviving high-connectivity incumbents. If they are on parallel trends before 2012, Katz's concern weakens substantially.
