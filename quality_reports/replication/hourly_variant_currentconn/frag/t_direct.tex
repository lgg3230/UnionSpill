     \textit{Notes:} This table presents difference-in-differences estimates of the ultractivity reform's direct effects (equation (\ref{eq:dir_spec})) using the current recomputable connectivity measure from \texttt{Data/RAIS\_aux/connectivity\_treat\_2007\_2011\_agg.dta}. An establishment is directly treated if it had a firm-level CBA filed before September 25, 2012 with a contractual end date after that date. Panel~A restricts the comparison group to untreated establishments with \texttt{totaltreat\_pw\_n\_current == 0}; Panel~B includes all untreated establishments. The final row reports the $p$-value of the test that the Post $\times$ Treatment effect is equal across the two panels. The test is estimated from a single stacked regression of the two samples in which each copy receives its own complete set of fixed effects and the difference-in-differences term is fully interacted with a copy indicator, so that the interaction coefficient equals the difference between the two panels. Standard errors are clustered at the establishment level, so that an establishment entering both copies forms a single cluster, which recovers the covariance between the two estimators and yields a valid test despite the overlapping samples. Wages and employment are measured in December of each year. Clause counts are observed only when a CBA is filed, reducing the number of observations in column~(4). Post $\times$ Treatment reports the average effect for 2012--2016, with 2011 as the reference year. Pre-trend (placebo) reports the coefficient from a placebo regression estimated on pre-treatment data only (2009--2010 relative to 2011). All specifications include establishment fixed effects and year or CBA-period fixed effects interacted with two-digit industry, microregion, and negotiation-month indicators and with quartile bins of pre-treatment firm size, per-worker flows, and the outcome. Standard errors clustered at the establishment level in parentheses. *** p$<$0.01, ** p$<$0.05, * p$<$0.10. Pre-treatment mean is the mean of the dependent variable over 2009--2011 in the estimation sample of the corresponding column.
