# CBA Similarity Results — Economist Panel Discussion
*April 21, 2026*

## Context

Five senior labor economists comment on the full set of results from the UnionSpill project, focusing on the CBA similarity findings and the core theoretical puzzle: why do directly treated firms (affected by Súmula 277) gain new CBA clauses, while spillover firms (untreated firms connected via worker flows) only show increased CBA similarity without clause additions — yet both show wage increases?

---

## The Full Results Summary

### Direct effects (treated firms — CBAs made evergreen by Súmula 277)
- Wages: significant increase
- Employment: null effect
- Number of CBA clauses: **+1.56** (SE 0.30, p<0.01) — wage clauses +0.28, employment clauses +0.38, other clauses +0.88
- Wage clause proportion: insignificant
- Employment clause proportion: insignificant
- Other clause proportion: **−0.010** (significant decrease)
- Firm survival: **+1.3–1.7 pp** (treated incumbents survive better)

### Spillover effects (untreated firms connected via pre-treatment worker flows)
- Wages: significant increase
- Employment: null effect
- Number of CBA clauses: **null** (0.018, SE 0.12)
- All clause type counts and proportions: null
- CBA cosine similarity to connected treated firm: **+0.013** (SE 0.004, p<0.01)
- Bray-Curtis similarity: **+0.008** (SE 0.003, p<0.01)
- Total variation similarity: **+0.010** (SE 0.003, p<0.01)
- Jaccard similarity: **+0.010** (SE 0.003, p<0.01)
- Average similarity to ALL treatment firms: cosine **+0.003** (p<0.01), others p<0.01–0.05
- Firm survival of connected untreated incumbents: **−1.1 pp**

## Prof. Marchetti — Union/Collective Bargaining Specialist

The results cohere once you distinguish between what a union can *demand* and what it can *credibly threaten*.

Súmula 277 changed the threat point. Before the reform, an expired CBA meant reversion to statutory minimums — a brutal outside option for workers. After, the status quo contract becomes workers' fallback. This is a textbook improvement in the union's disagreement payoff. The clause decomposition is especially telling: the largest absolute gain is in "other" clauses (+0.88), which in the Brazilian CBA literature typically cover procedural rights, enforcement mechanisms, and benefits — exactly what unions push for when they finally have leverage to expand *beyond* pure wage demands. That the *proportion* of wage clauses doesn't rise (even as counts do) tells you the union is broadening contractual scope, not just loading up on wage items.

At untreated firms: unions in Brazil (sindicatos de categoria) negotiate with multiple firms in the same jurisdiction simultaneously. When a subset — the treated ones — wins a richer contract, that outcome becomes a reference point for the next negotiation at untreated firms. But untreated firms' CBAs are not evergreen; the threat point for those workers didn't shift. Unions can *argue* from analogy but cannot *threaten* from the same position.

The result is *informal pattern bargaining through information diffusion*: similarity increases (the reference point pulls untreated CBAs toward treated ones on the **existing** margin) but clause counts do not rise (because the union cannot credibly force the **extensive** expansion that ultractivity enabled directly). The wage increase at spillover firms is then best understood as operating through the same reference-point channel — wage floors get renegotiated upward by analogy even without a structural change in institutional leverage.

The survival asymmetry adds a sobering footnote. Treated firms survive better — stronger contracts did not impose crushing costs, perhaps because firms already strong enough to be unionized could absorb the new obligations. Spillover firms see survival fall. Weaker firms on the margin, now facing wage pressure from the reference-point channel but without any direct institutional change justifying that pressure to their workers, find themselves squeezed. They cannot easily push back against a benchmark they did not technically sign onto, yet the labor market around them has shifted.

**Key contribution:** The reform created a two-tier transmission — an *institutional channel* for directly treated firms (genuine expansion of contract scope) and an *informational/imitation channel* for connected untreated firms (convergence toward a richer template, without the underlying leverage to enforce new scope). That distinction — institutional versus informational transmission — has real implications for how far and how fast reform effects propagate.

## Prof. Oliveira — Labor Market Competition / Outside Options

The null on CBA clause counts at untreated firms is the crucial identifying fact. If the transmission mechanism were running through formal bargaining — unions emboldened by gains at treated firms negotiating harder — you would expect clause counts to rise. They do not. What you observe is a shift in *content* similarity without a shift in *quantity*. That asymmetry is hard to square with a union learning story. Unions do not typically bargain for fewer, better-targeted clauses when they gain leverage; they bargain for more of everything. The null on quantity rules out the straightforward spillover-through-collective-bargaining channel.

What fits instead is a **competitive wage pressure mechanism operating through worker mobility** — an outside options channel. The intensive margin result is the key: firms with *more* workers flowing to and from treated firms — not just any connection — see larger wage increases. A worker who has recently moved from a treated firm, or who could plausibly move to one, arrives at an untreated firm with a credibly higher reservation wage. Employers respond by raising wages to retain that worker — not by renegotiating the CBA, but through wage-setting within whatever formal or informal discretion they already have.

The CBA similarity result is consistent with this reading and adds texture. Untreated firms are not copying clauses — they are adjusting the *implementation* of existing provisions in the direction of what treated firms do. That is the alignment you get from market pressure, not from union diffusion.

The survival asymmetry confirms this. Treated incumbents survive better. Untreated connected incumbents survive at lower rates (−1.1 pp). This is consistent with a competitive squeeze: they face higher labor costs without the legal protection or bargaining infrastructure to share the adjustment burden with workers through formal agreements.

**Key contribution:** The null on clause counts is not a puzzle — it is the **signature of the outside-options channel**. Workers carry reservation wages, not union contracts.

## Prof. Santos — Information and Norms Diffusion

The similarity-without-count pattern rules out simple imitation. If untreated firms were copying treated CBAs wholesale, clause counts would rise. What you observe is *selective recomposition*: untreated firms rearrange the internal structure of their CBAs to more closely resemble the treated firm's distribution across clause types, without adding net new clauses. This is the signature of **constrained norm adoption** under a binding contract margin. The untreated firm faces a different institutional environment — no ultractivity, weaker union leverage — so it cannot expand the CBA frontier. But within the feasible set, it reorganizes toward the portfolio it observes at the treated firm. In information diffusion terms: *selective salience transfer*, not wholesale copying.

Workers who move from treated to untreated firms carry three types of information simultaneously:
1. **Content knowledge** — which clauses were contested and what the language looks like
2. **Outcome knowledge** — which provisions actually bound in practice
3. **Expectation anchoring** — their reference point for acceptable compensation has shifted

Management at the receiving firm learns through hiring conversations, retention negotiations, and grievance patterns. This is diffusion through the employment relationship itself, not through any formal intermediary.

The fact that **bilateral** similarity (to the specifically connected firm) exceeds **average** similarity (to all treated firms) is powerful. It rules out sector-wide diffusion or industry norm updating — it is specific to the actual workers who moved. If this were broad sectoral learning, the two similarity measures would converge. That bilateral dominates is consistent with a *dyadic transmission* model: information is tied to the specific employment relationship, travels with the worker, and influences the receiving firm's CBA negotiation.

Why do wages respond when formal clauses do not? Wages can increase through informal adjustment that never appears in the CBA text. The reference wage carried by a mobile worker is a threat-point update operating *outside* the formal contract. The untreated firm adjusts actual wages without renegotiating clause structure because clause renegotiation requires union leverage it does not have, while wage adjustment requires only bilateral accommodation.

**Key contribution:** Information diffuses; power does not. The bilateral-vs-average similarity gap is a clean empirical test of *specific* worker-carried information vs. *broad* industry norm diffusion, and it points firmly to the former.

## Prof. Chen — Firm Dynamics and Selection

The survival asymmetry is not paradoxical — it reflects the difference between a **productivity-enhancing institutional change** and a **competitive cost shock**.

For treated firms, Súmula 277 is not just a wage mandate. The +1.56 additional clauses likely encode productivity-relevant provisions — job security rules that reduce turnover costs, training requirements, workforce stabilization. If these provisions raise worker effort or reduce costly churn, the wage increase can be offset by productivity gains. The reform may also raise the effective fixed cost of firm dissolution, mechanically reducing measured exit. Neither channel operates for spillover firms. They face wage pressure through labor market competition without any of the complementary productivity investments that the clauses encode.

The null employment effect, conditional on survival, is informative precisely because it tells you **adjustment happens through survival, not through within-firm labor substitution**. Surviving firms at both types sit above the exit margin; for them labor demand elasticity is low enough that the wage increase doesn't trigger downsizing. The margin of adjustment is the exit of marginal untreated firms.

From a Melitz-Hopenhayn perspective, the reform is doing something interesting: it raises the productivity cutoff for untreated firms while simultaneously raising the productivity of treated firms (through the clause-productivity channel). This is not standard selection where a cost shock drives out low-productivity firms symmetrically. Here treated firms' productivity distribution shifts right, while the exit margin for untreated firms shifts up. Whether this is efficient depends entirely on whether the clause-productivity gains are real or rent transfers. If real, the reallocation improves aggregate productivity. If rents, aggregate output falls while the wage bill redistributes.

**Key methodological concern:** The balanced-panel wage estimates for connected firms are identified on *surviving* connected firms — the strongest untreated firms, above the new higher exit margin. Their wage increase is a **lower bound** on the true average treatment effect of spillover connectivity, because firms that exited faced even larger wage pressures. The survival results — which are not subject to this truncation — are arguably the more reliable measure of the reform's competitive bite. The paper's wage estimates for connected firms should be read as conservative.

## Prof. Ferreira-Neto — Institutional Synthesis

### The institutional asymmetry in the bargaining game

Ultractivity does not merely improve outcomes for unionized workers — it fundamentally alters the threat point. Under the old regime, the expiration of a CBA returned both parties to the statutory floor, giving employers strong incentives to delay or walk away from negotiations. Súmula 277 replaced that employer-favorable outside option with the status quo contract, converting what had been a war of attrition into a negotiation over *improvements* to an already-binding baseline. This is a shift in the reference point of the Nash bargaining problem, not merely a change in worker bargaining weight.

The clause results follow directly. When the default becomes the existing contract, unions at treated firms can credibly demand expansions — new clauses covering additional contingencies, stronger enforcement provisions, novel employment protections — because the cost of disagreement is now borne symmetrically (or even more heavily by the employer). The +1.56 clauses, concentrated in employment and residual categories, reflect exactly this: unions using the improved threat point to formalize new contractual territory. Untreated firms face no such shift. Their negotiations remain anchored to the statutory floor as the disagreement outcome.

### Pattern bargaining transmits norms, not leverage

Sector unions in Brazil frequently cover both treated and untreated firms in the same region-industry cell. The treated-firm CBA, now richer and institutionally durable, becomes a reference contract — a focal point in the untreated firm's own upcoming negotiation. The union can successfully insist on matching *existing* clauses (producing similarity) but cannot demand *new* clauses, because the threat point for the untreated firm has not changed. **Pattern bargaining transmits the level of the norm, not the institutional leverage that generated it.** The bilateral-vs-average similarity gap reflects the dyadic specificity of this channel — it is the *connected* treated firm's CBA that anchors the norm, not an abstract sector average.

### Two distinct wage channels

At treated firms, wages rise through a direct bargaining channel enabled by the improved threat point. At untreated firms, wages rise through an imitation channel — existing wage provisions in untreated CBAs are re-anchored to a more generous benchmark during renewal, even without new clauses. These mechanisms are mechanically distinct — one is a change in the bargaining game, the other is a change in the bargaining norm — and the null clause count at untreated firms is precisely what distinguishes them empirically.

### Why employment is null everywhere, and what survival reveals

Rising wages without falling employment is consistent with lower turnover costs and productivity effects within surviving firms. But the survival results resolve the puzzle asymmetrically. Treated firms survive better: stronger contracts reduce hold-up risk, improve long-run investment incentives, and attract more productive workers. Untreated firms survive worse: they absorb wage increases without the institutional durability that allows treated firms to plan around them, and without the clause additions that might improve operational flexibility. The employment null within firms and the survival divergence across firms are jointly explained by the same mechanism.

### The unified three-step narrative

**Step 1 — Bargaining reform:** Súmula 277 shifts the threat point at treated firms, enabling both direct wage gains and clause expansion, while simultaneously improving contractual stability and survival prospects.

**Step 2 — Norm diffusion without leverage transfer:** Sector unions use the strengthened treated-firm contract as a reference in negotiations at untreated firms, producing convergence in CBA content (similarity ↑) and wage norms (wages ↑) without producing the clause expansion that requires the institutional leverage untreated firms lack.

**Step 3 — Asymmetric adjustment on the extensive margin:** Both firm types absorb higher wages without cutting employment among survivors, but the survival margin diverges — treated firms, backed by institutional durability, survive more; untreated firms, absorbing spillover costs without corresponding contractual protections, exit more. The reform improves outcomes within the formal unionized sector while selectively culling the weakest firms in the connected periphery.

## Synthesis

The five views are complementary rather than contradictory. The core theory:

### Two asymmetric transmission channels operating simultaneously

**1. Institutional channel (treated firms):** Ultractivity shifts the bargaining threat point, enabling genuine scope expansion (new clauses) and wage increases backed by formal contracts. The institutional durability also reduces uncertainty and hold-up risk, which is why treated firms survive *better* despite higher costs.

**2. Market + information channel (connected untreated firms):** Worker mobility carries both a *competitive signal* (raised reservation wages that firms must meet informally) and an *informational signal* (knowledge of what the treated CBA contains). These two signals together produce: wages rising through informal accommodation **outside** the formal CBA structure, and CBA content converging through pattern bargaining toward the treated firm's contract **within** the feasible (no-new-clause) set.

### Resolving the central puzzle

The puzzle — clauses rise at treated firms but only similarity rises at untreated firms, while wages rise at both — is resolved by this distinction:

- **Clause expansion** requires institutional leverage (changed threat point). Only treated firms have it.
- **CBA content convergence** requires only a reference point. Connected untreated firms have that (the treated firm's richer CBA).
- **Wage increases** require only competitive pressure or a shifted wage norm. Both types of firms face one or both.

The union plays a *partial* role in a precise sense: it transmits the norm (producing similarity) but cannot transfer the institutional foundation that produced the norm (so clause counts don't move). It is the vehicle for information, not for power.

The survival asymmetry is the cleanest summary statistic of the whole mechanism: treated firms internalized the reform's full institutional package and thrived; untreated firms absorbed only the cost side and the marginal ones exited.
