\textit{Notes:} This table compares spillover effects of layer-level connectivity to treated firms across three specifications and three worker-partitioning schemes. All regressions are restricted to untreated firms in the balanced firm panel and, for layer-level specifications, to firms with positive employment in all layers in all years 2009--2016. Connectivity is scaled to the 90th percentile of the control sample at 2009 and is interacted with a post-2012 indicator (S\'{u}mula 277 reform). The pre-trend (placebo) coefficient tests whether connectivity predicts differential outcomes in 2009--2010 relative to 2011, using only pre-reform years (2009--2011). The pre-trend F-test $p$-value tests the joint significance of all pre-trend event-study coefficients; small values indicate detectable pre-trends.

\textit{Outcome levels.} Columns (1), (4), (7) and (2), (5), (8) use \textit{layer-level} outcomes: each observation is a firm--layer--year cell, and the connectivity measure captures the share of that layer's outflows reaching treated firms. Columns (3), (6), (9) use \textit{firm-level} outcomes and the aggregate firm-level connectivity, restricted to the subset of firms present in the layer sample. Layer-level hourly wages are omitted in this balanced-layer version, so the corresponding within-firm and cross-firm cells are blank.

\textit{Fixed effects.} Columns (1), (4), (7) (within-firm) absorb firm$\times$year FE; identification comes from cross-layer variation within the same firm--year. Columns (2), (5), (8) (cross-firm) include firm-level FE: microregion$\times$year, industry$\times$year, and mode$\times$year, controlling for local labor-market trends, sector shocks, and contract-type composition. Columns (3), (6), (9) (firm-level) include firm FE and year FE.

\textit{Layer definitions.} Panel A partitions workers by 2-bin education: no high-school diploma (no HS) versus completed high school or more. Panel B partitions workers by gender (female / male). Panel C classifies workers as non-white or white.

Standard errors clustered at the firm level. $^{***}p<0.01$, $^{**}p<0.05$, $^{*}p<0.10$.
