\begin{tablenotes}
\item \textit{Notes:} This table presents difference-in-differences estimates of the direct effects of Brazil's 2012 ultractivity reform on establishment employment flow counts, scaled by each establishment's own pretreatment (2009--2011) average of the same count (firm-scale specification). A coefficient of 0.10 indicates the count rose by 10\% relative to the establishment's own pretreatment baseline. Columns~(1)--(5) report firm-scaled hires, total separations, layoffs, quits, and churn count (= hires + separations). Columns~(6)--(7) report firm-scaled within-year retention count (January stayers surviving to December) and year-over-year retention count (December-to-December survivors). Columns~(8)--(9) report firm-scaled total contracted hours and December employment. Establishments with a zero pretreatment average for a given count are excluded from that outcome's regression. Mean (2011) reports the unscaled raw count mean for reference. The reform, effective September~25, 2012, extended existing collective bargaining agreements (CBAs) indefinitely upon expiration, increasing the bargaining power of workers at treated establishments---those with an active CBA on that date. The sample covers Brazilian establishments in the Lagos sample observed annually from 2009 to 2016. Post $\times$ Treatment measures the average treatment effect for 2012--2016; Pre $\times$ Treatment is a placebo test using 2009--2011 as the pre-reform period. All regressions include establishment fixed effects, year fixed effects interacted with two-digit industry, microregion, and negotiation-month indicators, and quartile-bin controls for pre-treatment per-worker pairwise worker flows (2007--2011) and pre-treatment establishment size, interacted with year fixed effects. Panel~A restricts the control group to establishments with zero pre-reform worker-flow connectivity to treated firms; Panel~B to establishments with at most 1\% connectivity; Panel~C includes all untreated establishments. Standard errors clustered at the establishment level in parentheses. *** p$<$0.01, ** p$<$0.05, * p$<$0.10.
