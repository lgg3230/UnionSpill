\begin{tablenotes}
\item \textit{Notes:} This table presents difference-in-differences estimates of the direct effects of Brazil's 2012 ultractivity reform on establishment employment flow rates and worker mobility outcomes. Columns (1)--(6) report the retention rate (Jan stayers / Dec employment), hiring rate, separation rate (= separations / average employment), quit rate, layoff rate, and churn rate (= (separations + hires) / average employment). Column (7) reports log total contracted hours summed across December-employment workers. Columns (8)--(10) report annual per-worker bilateral flow outcomes: total bilateral flows per worker, total outflows per worker, and total inflows per worker. These three columns measure whether the reform reshapes overall labour mobility. The reform, effective September~25, 2012, extended existing collective bargaining agreements (CBAs) indefinitely upon expiration, increasing the bargaining power of workers at treated establishments---those with an active CBA on that date. The sample covers Brazilian establishments in the Lagos sample observed annually from 2009 to 2016. Post $\times$ Treatment measures the average treatment effect for 2012--2016; Pre $\times$ Treatment is a placebo test using 2009--2011 as the pre-reform period. All regressions include establishment fixed effects, year fixed effects interacted with two-digit industry, microregion, and negotiation-month indicators, and quartile-bin controls for pre-treatment per-worker pairwise worker flows (2007--2011) and pre-treatment establishment size, interacted with year fixed effects. Panel~A restricts the control group to establishments with zero pre-reform worker-flow connectivity to treated firms; Panel~B to establishments with at most 1\% connectivity; Panel~C includes all untreated establishments. Standard errors clustered at the establishment level in parentheses. *** p$<$0.01, ** p$<$0.05, * p$<$0.10.
