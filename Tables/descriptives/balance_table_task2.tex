\item \textit{Notes:} Each cell reports a single regression coefficient with its robust standard error in parentheses, on the 2011 pre-treatment cross-section (one observation per establishment). In columns (1)--(6) the coefficient is on a treated indicator (the treated--control difference in the row characteristic); in columns (7)--(9) it is the slope on establishment connectivity within the spillover control sample (untreated establishments only). Each comparison is shown at three control levels. \textit{Raw}: no controls. \textit{Main}: the controls common to every main-specification regression---two-digit industry, microregion, and negotiation-month indicators, and quartile-bin dummies for pre-treatment per-worker pairwise worker flows (2007--2011) and pre-treatment log employment (2009--2010). \textit{$+$Own}: \textit{Main} plus quartile-bin dummies of the row characteristic's own pre-treatment level, mirroring the main specification's own-outcome control. The own window is 2007--2010 for log wages and log employment (reconstructed pre-period data) and 2009--2010 otherwise; it always excludes the 2011 value being compared. Own quartiles are four group indicators, not the lagged level, so they coarsely adjust for baseline differences rather than mechanically absorbing the outcome. Connectivity is a treatment-exposure gradient rather than an outcome, so its row uses the common controls only ($+$Own $=$ Main); the worker-flows row's own quartile is the common flows quartile. Comparing \textit{Main} against \textit{$+$Own} shows how much of the residual balance comes from conditioning a characteristic on its own prior level. Columns (1)--(3) compare treated establishments to all untreated controls; columns (4)--(6) restrict controls to establishments with zero pre-reform connectivity. Tenure and age are in years; connectivity is normalized to the 90th percentile of the spillover sample in 2009. Standard errors are heteroskedasticity-robust. *** $p<.01$, ** $p<.05$, * $p<.10$.
