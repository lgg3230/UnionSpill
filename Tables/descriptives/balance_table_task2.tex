\item \textit{Notes:} Each cell reports a single regression coefficient with its robust standard error in parentheses, using the 2011 pre-treatment cross-section (one observation per establishment). In columns (1)--(4) the coefficient is on a treated indicator, so it is the treated--control difference in the row characteristic; in columns (5)--(6) it is the slope on establishment connectivity within the spillover control sample (untreated establishments only). Columns (1), (3), and (5) are raw; columns (2), (4), and (6) partial out the paper's main-specification controls. Mirroring that specification, which absorbs each outcome's own pre-treatment quartile together with an employment quartile, the controlled columns condition on a common set applied to every row---two-digit industry, microregion, and negotiation-month indicators, quartile-bin dummies for pre-treatment per-worker pairwise worker flows (2007--2011), and quartile-bin dummies for pre-treatment log employment (2009--2010)---plus, for each characteristic, quartile-bin dummies of that characteristic's own 2009--2010 mean. These are four group indicators, not the lagged level, so they coarsely adjust for baseline differences rather than mechanically absorbing the outcome; and the 2009--2010 window excludes the 2011 value being compared. The employment row's own quartile is the common employment quartile, and the worker-flows row's own quartile is the common flows quartile; connectivity is a treatment-exposure gradient rather than an outcome, so its row uses the common controls only. Columns (1)--(2) compare treated establishments to all untreated controls; columns (3)--(4) restrict controls to establishments with zero pre-reform connectivity. The contrast between the raw and controlled columns shows whether the specification brings the samples closer to balance: large raw imbalances that collapse toward zero once controls are applied indicate the covariates absorb the relevant selection. Tenure and age are in years. Connectivity is normalized to the 90th percentile of the spillover sample in 2009. Standard errors are heteroskedasticity-robust. *** $p<.01$, ** $p<.05$, * $p<.10$.
